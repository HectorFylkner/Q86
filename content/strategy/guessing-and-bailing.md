# Guessing and Bailing

A guess is a move, not a failure. Made deliberately and early it costs one
question. Made after three minutes of sunk cost it costs three.

## Deliberate versus panicked

The difference is entirely about when.

A **deliberate guess** happens at the sixty-second decision point, when you
have established that you have no path. You eliminate what you can, pick,
and leave with the clock intact. On an adaptive section this is close to
free: one question, and the bank you preserved buys accuracy on the next
three.

A **panicked guess** happens at minute four, when you have burned the
question *and* the time, and then answer anyway. You paid full price and
got the same outcome. Worse, the next two questions get rushed, so the true
cost is three questions, not one.

If you tag a miss as "guess", the honest follow-up question is *which one
was it*. They want different fixes: a deliberate guess that missed is a
content gap to diagnose; a panicked guess is a pacing failure.

## Eliminating before you pick

Even with no path, the choices carry information:

- **Order of magnitude.** If the stem multiplies two two-digit numbers, a
  single-digit answer is not it. Estimate crudely and cut.
- **Parity and units digit.** A sum of an even count of consecutive
  integers, a units digit forced by a power's cycle — these often eliminate
  two choices in five seconds without solving anything.
- **The obvious trap.** In a multi-step question, the value you would get
  by stopping one step early is almost always listed. If one choice is
  suspiciously reachable, it is probably the planted one.
- **Answer-choice structure.** Five choices where four share a form and one
  does not; a pair that differ only in sign. The odd one out is rarely the
  key, and a sign pair usually means the question is testing exactly that
  sign.

Three eliminated choices turn a $20\%$ guess into a $50\%$ one. That is
worth fifteen seconds and never worth ninety.

## After the section

Guesses should be reviewed first, before the misses you understand. A guess
is the only miss that carries no diagnostic information at all — you do not
yet know what you did not know. Work the question untimed, find the rule
that was missing, and retag it as the content gap it actually was. Then
take that cure.

A section with two deliberate guesses and clean pacing is a good section.
A section with zero guesses and three time sinks is not.
