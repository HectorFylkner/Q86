# Reading Your Misses

Every wrong answer is one of six things, and the six want six different
cures. Guessing which one you are looking at is the difference between a
post-mortem that changes your score and one that just makes you feel bad.
Tag honestly — the tag is the prescription.

## Content gap

*You did not know the rule.* Not "I forgot under pressure" — you would not
have produced the method with unlimited time.

The cure is the chapter, and often not the one you think. A content gap in
mixtures is usually a ratios gap; a gap in remainders is usually a
divisibility gap. Reread the prerequisite chapter first, then the chapter
for the subtopic itself, then retake the chapter test. Do not drill the
subtopic until the test clears: drilling a rule you do not have teaches you
only that you do not have it.

## Setup error

*You knew the rule and pointed it at the wrong thing.* The translation from
English to algebra broke: the wrong base for a percent, the wrong variable
isolated, the wrong quantity called $x$.

The cure is cues, not content. Reread the chapter's trigger-cue list —
phrase on the left, method on the right — and then the Algebraic
translation chapter, which is where every setup error eventually lives.
Setup errors repeat with uncanny fidelity, so put the cue list in the deck
and let it come back at you for a week.

## Calculation error

*The setup was right and the arithmetic betrayed you.* This is the miss
that feels least serious and costs the most points, because it scales with
every question you attempt.

The cure is the chapter's speed moves plus the pattern trainer. Speed moves
are legitimate shortcuts that remove steps — and a step you never take is a
step you cannot fumble. Compress the chain before you touch dollars; pick
convenient numbers; divide straight from the relation. Then run pattern
rounds until the fundamentals are automatic.

## Misread

*You solved a question the test did not ask.* You found the width when it
wanted the perimeter, the balance when it wanted the interest, the number
of blue marbles when it wanted red.

The cure is procedural, not mathematical: reread the final sentence of the
stem before you select. Every good trap map on this platform lists the
quantity you would have computed by misreading — the near-miss is always a
listed choice, deliberately. Build the habit of circling the target noun
before you start work, and check what you circled before you answer.

## Time pressure

*You knew how, and the clock took it.* You would have been right at ninety
seconds and you were at two hundred and forty.

The cure is pacing, and the fix is almost never "go faster." It is
"bail earlier." Compare the attempt against its difficulty benchmark: a
question that ran half again over its benchmark is a time sink, and the
skill you need is recognizing one at sixty seconds rather than at two
hundred. Read the pacing note, then run timed sets — untimed practice
cannot train a timing failure.

## Guess

*You had nothing and picked.* Distinguish this from time pressure: a guess
means you had no path, not that you ran out of road.

A guess is a content gap that has not been diagnosed yet. Go find which
rule was missing, tag *that* as the content gap, and take the chapter cure.
Then read the guessing note: on an adaptive exam a guess is a legitimate
move when it is deliberate and cheap, and a disaster when it is a panic
after three minutes of sunk cost.
