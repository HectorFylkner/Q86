# Reading the Stem

A misread is not a careless error. It is a predictable one, it is planted
for, and it can be trained out with a procedure.

## The trap map is not decoration

Every question in this bank carries a trap map: one line per wrong choice
naming the mistake that choice was built to catch. Read them after a miss,
especially when you were confident. If your answer appears there with a
description of exactly what you did, you did not have bad luck — you walked
into a designed hole.

The most common entry, across the whole bank, is some version of *answers a
different quantity than the one asked for*: the width instead of the
perimeter, the balance instead of the interest, the multiplier $k$ instead
of the count it multiplies. That distractor is present in nearly every
multi-step question because the error is that common.

## The procedure

Three habits, in order:

1. **Circle the target noun before you start.** Not the numbers — the
   thing being asked for, and its units. "How many *dimes*." "In
   *dollars*." "The *perimeter*." Ten seconds.
2. **Note what the question does *not* say.** "Integer" is the word most
   often absent on purpose; so is "distinct", "positive", and "nonzero".
   Parity rules need integers. Squares need nonzero to be positive. A
   median needs sorting. If a constraint you are about to assume is not
   written down, it is not there.
3. **Reread the final sentence before selecting.** After the arithmetic,
   before the click. This is the single highest-yield five seconds in the
   section, because it catches the error at the only moment it is still
   free.

## Reversals worth memorizing

Certain phrases invert in ways that read as symmetric and are not:

- "$a$ less than $b$" is $b - a$ — the phrase reverses the written order.
- "Profit is $k\%$ of the *selling price*" is not "$k\%$ of the cost."
  Different equations, different answers, both listed.
- "$x$ is $25\%$ greater than $y$" makes $y$ only $20\%$ less than $x$. The
  base is whatever follows "than".
- "Between $a$ and $b$" usually excludes the endpoints; "from $a$ through
  $b$" includes them.
- "More than" is strict. Hitting the target exactly does not clear it.

Each of these has a chapter cue and a trap-gallery entry. If you have
misread the same one twice, it belongs in your deck, not in your memory.
