# Positioning

## One sentence

Q86 är en svensk träningsplattform för GMAT Focus kvantdel, där
undervisningen är på svenska och frågorna på engelska — precis som på
provet.

**In English, for anyone who needs it:** Q86 is a Swedish training
platform for the GMAT Focus quant section: instruction in Swedish,
questions in English, exactly as the exam presents them.

## Who it is for

**The person this is built for:** a Swedish-speaking candidate, 23–33,
applying to a master's or an MBA where GMAT is required or weighs heavily.
They have a degree, often a job, and between four and twelve weeks. They
are not bad at maths — they got into a Swedish business programme — but
they have never met GMAT's particular way of being wrong.

Three things are true of almost all of them:

1. **They have done Högskoleprovet**, and expect the quant section to be a
   repeat. It is not: no geometry, English wording where a single word
   decides the answer, distractors built from named mistakes, and two
   minutes eight seconds a question.
2. **Their bottleneck is diagnosis, not volume.** They can do more
   questions. What they cannot do is say why the last ten were wrong.
3. **They are short on time and long on anxiety.** A platform that adds
   decisions is a platform they abandon in week two.

**Who it is not for:** people who need verbal or Data Insights (Q86 does
one section), people who want a tutor, people looking for official
practice tests (that is GMAC's, and only GMAC's, to sell).

## The problem, in the words a candidate would use

> Jag gör frågor och får fel, men jag vet inte varför. Ibland kan jag inte,
> ibland ställer jag upp fel, ibland räknar jag fel, ibland läser jag fel,
> ibland hinner jag inte. Alla fem känns likadana efteråt, och rådet är
> alltid "öva mer".

That is the whole product thesis. Six failure modes, six different fixes,
and no ordinary study method distinguishes them.

## What Q86 does about it

**Every miss gets a name before you move on.** Six error types — content
gap, setup error, calculation error, misread, time pressure, guess — one
click each, and the daily plan re-weights around what actually goes wrong.

Three supporting facts, each of which is checkable:

- **A verified bank.** Every question passes an authoring gate: the answer
  is recomputed numerically, each distractor must correspond to a named
  mistake, and a question that fails is never admitted. 360 questions
  today, and the number on the site is read from the database rather than
  typed into the copy.
- **Swedish instruction, English questions.** The boundary is absolute and
  it is enforced by a test, not a convention: stems, answer choices and
  Data Sufficiency statements stay in English; everything around them is
  Swedish.
- **A computed plan.** The same inputs give the same plan. It weighs
  accuracy per skill, an imported score report, and the days left.

## Positioning against the alternatives

| Alternative | What it does well | Where Q86 differs |
|---|---|---|
| Official GMAC material | The only real questions and the only real scoring | Q86 is practice between those, and says so |
| Large international platforms | Enormous banks, full three-section coverage | Q86 does one section, in Swedish, with named errors |
| Free question dumps | Free | Unverified: a wrong answer key teaches the wrong thing |
| A private tutor | Adapts to a person | Costs 20–40× per hour and cannot be opened at 23:00 |
| Högskoleprovet material | Familiar, Swedish | A different exam: geometry included, no adaptivity, Swedish wording |

**The honest version of the competitive claim:** Q86 is narrower than the
international platforms and cheaper than a tutor. Its argument is that a
Swede preparing for the quant section is better served by depth in one
section and instruction in their own language than by breadth they will
not use.

## Proof points that exist today

Only these. Everything else is aspiration.

- 360 verified questions, 24 concept chapters, 24 drillable subtopics.
- Six-type error taxonomy applied to every attempt.
- A free 12-question diagnostic that needs no account and stores nothing.
- Deterministic daily planning.
- Swedish interface, chapters and coaching; English questions.

## Proof points that do not exist yet

Say none of these until they are true:

- Any score improvement, for anyone.
- Any number of users, students, or "candidates trained".
- Any testimonial.
- Any comparison of results against another platform.

## Tone

Plain, specific, unexcited. The reader is a smart adult under time
pressure who has been marketed at by test-prep companies since they were
seventeen. Concrete numbers, named mechanisms, admitted limits. No
exclamation marks, no "unlock your potential", no "revolutionary".

The single best sentence to keep in mind when writing anything: *would I
send this to a friend who is taking the GMAT in six weeks?*
